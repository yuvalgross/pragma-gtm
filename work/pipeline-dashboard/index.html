Pipeline dashboard: what changed when outbound started from signals.

A revenue dashboard that puts the 12 months before a signal based outbound engine next to the 12 months after. Live buying signals come from Clay, deals and contacts live in a Supabase analytics schema, Hex reads them through its own locked down database user, and the app is public with the code hidden.

How it works

Find signalsThree Clay company searches on the ICP: hiring for sales, fast headcount growth and a new sales leader. A company with two or more signals goes on the call list.
ModelOne analytics schema in Supabase Postgres: Clay signals, deals, contacts, companies and outreach sequences. Every deal carries its lead source, period, stage path, amount and days to close.
Lock downHex logs in as its own database user. It sees the analytics schema and nothing else, so the app's tables with personal data stay out of reach.
QueryFive SQL queries: headline numbers, revenue by month, close rate by source, the funnel and outreach. Months only count once they are complete.
PublishA Hex app with the code hidden and a public view only link. Hex's agent can answer new questions on the same tables.

The charts, and what they say

Signal led deals close at 40%, generic outreach at 12%. Generic outreach still brings in the most money because it runs at the biggest volume: 325 closed deals against 50. The move is not to stop it, but to feed more of the pipeline from signals, where every closed deal is worth more effort.

Won revenue by month

Grey: the 12 months before. Orange: the 12 months after the signal engine went live. Hover a bar for the month. On a phone, swipe sideways.

Close rate by lead source

All closed deals. Referral closes best, as it usually does, but there are only 22 of them. Signal led matches it at more than twice the volume.

Before vs after

Two panels, one scale each. Revenue grew 32% on 39 to 50 won deals. The share of leads that became MQLs grew 42%.

Where signal led pipeline comes from

The 40% close rate only matters if there are enough signals to act on. Three Clay company searches on the ICP (software and e-learning companies with 51 to 500 staff, headquartered in Germany, Austria or Switzerland) each returned 20 companies: open sales or RevOps roles posted in the last 30 days, headcount up 20% or more in six months, and a new VP Sales, Head of Sales or CRO in the last three months. 52 companies in total. Most show one signal; six show two or three at once, and those go on the call list.

The data

Deals
482 closed deals over 24 months plus 28 open ones. Exact closed and won counts per period and source, so the close rates are 40% and 12% by construction. Won amounts are scaled so each 12 month period totals exactly €295k and €390k.
Contacts
1,000 leads, 500 per period. 100 MQLs before, 142 after: 20.0% to 28.4%, a 42% uplift.
Companies
300 invented companies with industry, size, region and ICP tier.
Clay signals
52 real companies with 60 signals from Clay company search, captured 23 November 2026. Public company data only: name, domain, location, size. No people, no contact data.
Outreach
Nine real Apollo sequences, aggregate counts only. Names replaced with a description of the audience and the steps.
Not in it
No real person or deal. The only real companies are the Clay signal accounts, and they appear as prospects, not customers. No figure from a current employer's CRM.

What I would change first

Replace the synthetic tables with a nightly sync from the CRM. An n8n workflow would pull closed and open deals from HubSpot into the same schema, so every chart and query stays as it is and the numbers refresh on their own.

Split signal led by signal type. Funding, hiring and job changes probably do not close at the same rate, and the next budget decision is which signal source to pay for. The same chart answers it with one more column.

How I would judge it: the sales lead opens it on Monday instead of asking for a spreadsheet. If nobody opens it for two weeks, it answers the wrong question.

How it was built

AI did
Claude Code wrote the SQL, the data generator and the chart code from my spec, and set up the Hex project through Hex's connector.
I did
Chose the story and the targets, decided what is real and what is synthetic, set the access rule that keeps the app tables out of Hex, anonymized the outreach data, and checked every number against the source tables.
Not built
The CRM step: pushing the call list into HubSpot as companies with the signals attached, and a nightly refresh of the Clay searches. The data is a snapshot from November 2026.
