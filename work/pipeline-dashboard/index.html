Which lead source deserves the next €10k?

The question: if a sales team has €10k more to spend on winning deals, which lead source should get it? My answer: signal-led outreach, where I contact companies that have just shown a buying signal, like hiring for sales or a new sales leader.

It beats generic outbound as long as pursuing one signal-led deal costs under about €1,670. That limit is modelled: the deals are made up and the costs are my assumptions.

What is real, what is built, what is not

In short: Clay finds companies showing buying signals, the deals and contacts sit in a Supabase database, and a Hex dashboard reads them through a restricted database user. The dashboard is public and the code is hidden. The 40% and 12% close rates are set in the made-up data on purpose, so the page shows how I would analyse and present them, not a discovery.

How it is put together

Find signalsThree Clay company searches on the ICP: hiring for sales, headcount growth and a new sales leader. A company with two or more signals goes on the call list.
ModelOne analytics schema in Supabase Postgres: Clay signals, deals, contacts, companies and outreach sequences. Every deal carries its lead source, period, stage path, amount and days to close.
Keep it safeThe dashboard tool (Hex) connects as its own database user. That user sees the analytics data and nothing else, so tables with personal data stay out of reach.
QueryFive SQL queries: headline numbers, revenue by month, close rate by source, the funnel and outreach. Months only count once they are complete.
PublishA Hex app with the code hidden and a public view only link. Hex's agent can answer new questions on the same tables.

Where signal led pipeline comes from

A 40% close rate only helps if there are enough signals to act on, so I went looking for them. The target is my ideal customer profile (ICP): corporate learning, L&D and HR tech companies that sell to other businesses, with 51 to 1,000 staff, based in DACH, Benelux, the UK, the Nordics, France or Spain.

Three Clay company searches looked for a buying signal: an open sales or RevOps role posted in the last 30 days, headcount up 5% or more in six months, or a new VP Sales, Head of Sales, Sales Director or CRO in the last six months. After removing recruiters, staffing agencies, schools and public bodies, 24 companies with 31 signals were left. Most show one signal. Seven show two at once, and those make this week's call list.

The data

Deals
482 closed deals over 24 months plus 28 open ones. Exact closed and won counts per period and source, so the 40% and 12% close rates are set by design, not discovered. Won amounts are scaled so each 12 month period totals exactly €295k and €390k.
Contacts
1,000 leads, 500 per period. 100 MQLs before, 142 after: 20.0% to 28.4%, a 42% uplift.
Companies
300 invented companies with industry, size, region and ICP tier.
Clay signals
24 real companies with 31 signals from Clay company search, captured 23 November 2026. Public company data only: name, domain, location, size. No people, no contact data.
Outreach
Nine real Apollo sequences, aggregate counts only. Names replaced with a description of the audience and the steps.
Not in it
No real person or deal. The only real companies are the Clay signal accounts, and they appear as prospects, not customers. No figure from a current employer's CRM.

What I would change first

Replace the synthetic tables with a nightly sync from the CRM. The n8n workflow for it is written (in the repo): it pulls closed and open deals from HubSpot into the same schema, so every chart and query stays as it is. I ran it end to end in n8n with sample deals and a mock database, which caught two bugs. It has not touched a real HubSpot account yet, so the page still reads synthetic deals.

Split signal led by signal type. Funding, hiring and job changes probably do not close at the same rate, and the next budget decision is which signal source to pay for. The same chart answers it with one more column.

How I would judge it: the sales lead opens it on Monday instead of asking for a spreadsheet. If nobody opens it for two weeks, it answers the wrong question.
