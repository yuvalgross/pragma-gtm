Work sample

Account prioritisation: which 80 accounts get a call this week, and what happens when the signals run out.

A weekly run for an outbound team of four SDRs selling B2B software. It reads 500 accounts and their buying signals, decides who a rep may call, scores the rest, routes them by territory and capacity, and gives every rep a Monday list with the reason to call, who to call and a first step. Leadership changes the rules in one control file, never in the code.

The answer: the limit is signals, not reps

Picking 80 accounts a week was the easy part. The run shows that the accounts with a real buying signal are used up in about two weeks. Adding SDR hours does not fix that. More signal sources do, and so does putting capacity where the signals are.

At the rate new signals arrived (6 accounts in the week before week 2), more than 60 of the 80 slots in week 3 would go to accounts with no reason to call now.

What the data was hiding

Each of these would have put a wrong list in front of a rep without any error message.

  • A third of the list disappears on load. The region code NA (North America) reads as an empty value in pandas by default. That would have dropped 177 accounts. The run reads it as text and logs it.
  • Signal labels are wrong. 34 single blog visits are filed as pricing visits. The score reads what a signal says, not the type it was filed under.
  • Some competitor renewals are the seller's own. 4 customers carry a competitor renewal signal while their current provider is the seller. That is a retention alert for the AE, not an SDR call.
  • The same company twice. 3 companies sit under two records with different spellings. One copy has sales history, the other looks brand new, so the same company landed on one rep's list twice. The run merges each pair into the record with history and logs it so a person can undo it.
  • Demand sits where nobody sells. 17 accounts in a region with no rep show a signal, and the best of them outscores everything on the reps' lists.

How an account is scored

Score = (signal points + fit points) × leadership boost

LevelPointsExamples
High: close to a decision18 to 30Contract renewal due, three pricing visits, unhappy with current provider, API docs
Medium: something is changing in their business10 to 22Hiring a platform engineer, new product launch, new head of procurement, funding
Low: weak or early8 to 10Platform release, new VP Engineering, webinar, whitepaper
Ignored0A single blog visit
  • Signals fade. Half the points every 21 days, nothing after 90.
  • Renewals work the other way. Full points while the date is 1 to 6 months away, half in the final month, nothing once it has passed.
  • One per type, plus a bonus for variety. Ten docs visits count as one. Each extra type of signal adds 6 points.
  • Funding counts double next to a build signal. Money on its own says nothing about a buying decision. Money within 60 days of an engineering job post says it is going into the product.
  • Fit, up to 29 points. Company size, current vendor (an in house build or several vendors scores highest) and industry. A missing value scores a small default, not zero, so a gap does not knock an account out.

How leadership stays in control

Leadership edits one file and reruns. The logic never changes.

TabWhat it changes
SignalsPoints for each signal, or switch one off
SettingsHow fast signals fade, the cool down, the minimum score, routing switches
BoostsPush a segment up or down, for example logistics × 1.5
AccountsTake an account off the list, or pin it to the top
SDRsA rep's capacity this week, or out of office

I tested both kinds of change. Excluding a rep's number one takes it off the list. Boosting one segment by 1.5 takes its accounts in week 1 from 4 to 6. A boost only reorders accounts inside a territory; to move effort between territories, change capacity.

What I would decide

I tried each option by changing the control file and rerunning.

  • Moving 8 weekly slots from LATAM to EMEA brings fit only accounts in week 2 from 19 to 12, and 148 signal accounts get worked over two weeks instead of 141.
  • No setting gets past about 150, because that is every signal account there is.
  • At 60 new accounts a week instead of 80, fit only work disappears and 20 slots a week are free for follow ups, AE alerts and the region with no rep.

My call: move rep time to EMEA now, make new signal sources the first build, and set the weekly pace once week 2 replies are in. The sources I would add first are specific to the buying moment rather than another intent feed: a competing product reaching end of support, public tech stack changes, and product sign ups matched to named accounts.

How it was built

I did
Every decision: which signals count and how much, the gate, the routing rules, the assumptions and the recommendation. I checked every number on this page against the output.
AI did
Claude Code wrote the Python from those decisions. No model classifies signals during the run: 77 signal values map to 28 readable rules, and a check confirms every value lands on one.
Checks
A separate script confirms what must never break: 80 accounts a week, every rep at capacity, no signal dated on or after its Monday, no customer or AE account on a list, no account on both weeks' lists, no company listed twice.
Not built
The Clay enrichment, the n8n writes into Salesforce and the Slack message. Designed, not running.
